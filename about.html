About / the person behind the pixels

I notice things.
Then I usually need to know why.

How I got here

Sociology first.
Design followed.

I didn’t start college planning to become a designer. I studied Sociology at UT Austin, which meant spending a lot of time thinking about people: why we behave the way we do, how systems shape our choices, and why something obvious to one person can feel completely unintuitive to another.

Eventually I realized that was also the part of design I cared about most. Not just making something look good — understanding why it wasn’t working in the first place.

Somehow, it all connects

I analyze.

I want to understand the behavior behind the problem before jumping to a solution.

I organize.

Messy information is strangely satisfying. Give me an overgrown Figma file, a complicated hierarchy, or twelve competing priorities.

I question.

“Because we’ve always done it that way” is useful context. It isn’t a design rationale.

I optimize.

I like the part after launch — the data, friction, and tiny changes that make the next version better.
