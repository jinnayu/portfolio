01 / The customer journey

Interest doesn’t always
mean readiness.

For an intimate health product, an email can arrive while someone is still deciding whether the device is relevant, how it works, or whether they feel comfortable buying it.

The lifecycle work connected those questions across a sequence. My role covered flow design, campaigns, and reusable email modules in Klaviyo, with a visual language connected to the website and product experience.

What does someone need to understand before the next step feels reasonable?

02 / Design the sequence

One message.
One clear job.

Recognize

Explain why this message is relevant to the recipient.

Understand

Introduce the product and address a specific question.

Reassure

Support the explanation with practical information.

Continue

Offer one clear primary next step.

The Figma file includes a six-email quiz flow, a browse-abandonment message, and a flash-sale email. Those different contexts make sequencing important: education, reminders, and time-sensitive offers should not all read like the same message.

03 / Three messages, three purposes

Let the question
shape the hierarchy.

Decision 01 / A visible primary action

Make the next step
easy to find.

Each message builds a clear path from headline to product imagery, supporting content, and a primary action. Education and reassurance support that action instead of competing with it.

Why the action repeats

The example emails repeat the shopping action after longer educational content. This provides another entry point once a reader has reached the information that helps them decide, without introducing a different competing task.

Decision 02 / Reusable content

Build modules for the
messages that keep coming.

The working file separates logo and title, hero imagery, offer blocks, product education, benefit icons, and footer content. Reuse made it possible to extend the flow without treating every send as a new layout.

Consistency across channels

The same product imagery and brand language connect the inbox to the website. That continuity supports the transition from reading an email to evaluating the product on a landing page.

05 / Reflection

A lifecycle flow should feel like a conversation that remembers where you are.

Strong email design depends on relevance and sequence as much as visual polish. A clear system makes it easier to keep the messages connected while giving each one a specific purpose.

For the next iteration, I would map performance to each customer question—relevance, understanding, or readiness—so the team could improve the sequence without reducing every decision to the same click metric.
