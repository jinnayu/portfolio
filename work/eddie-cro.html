02 / Eddie · Landing pages & experimentation

Different stories.
Better questions.

Ego-led or partner-led: which story actually gets people to buy?

My roleUX/UI Designer
FocusCampaign narrative · responsive layouts · testing
DeliverableEgo, partner, efficacy & value directions
01 Context02 Approach03 Decisions04 Outcome05 Reflection

01 / The problem

A persuasive story
depends on who’s listening.

Eddie’s landing pages had to introduce an unfamiliar, intimate health product and help visitors decide whether to take a quiz or consider a purchase. A single message could not answer every motivation equally well.

The design work explored four narrative directions—ego, partner, efficacy, and value—and different page lengths. My role was to translate those directions into distinct, responsive experiences that could be evaluated rather than chosen on preference alone.

Does personal confidence or a shared relationship story create a more compelling reason to act?

02 / Designing the comparison

Change the story.
Keep the question visible.

MESSAGE & IMAGE

The ego page addresses the individual. The partner page leads with a couple and relationship framing. These are distinct campaign propositions, not a clean test of a single headline.

THE NEXT STEP

Both designs include a direct product action and a question-based route. The page needs to support people ready to buy and people still deciding whether the device is relevant.

SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

Benefits, use guidance, testimonials, and reassurance build on the opening story. The first impression and the rest of the page need to make the same promise.

03 / Experimental thinking

A better-looking variant
is still a hypothesis.

Frame

Name the decision: story, page length, imagery, or action.

Design

Make the treatment distinct enough to evaluate.

Compare

Read behavior against the relevant conversion goal.

Interpret

Keep audience, traffic, and page context attached to the result.

Decision / Information depth

How much explanation
does this visitor need?

The Figma file contains long and short versions across desktop, tablet, and mobile. That makes information depth a separate design question from the campaign’s opening message.

Why isolate this question?

A long page can answer more objections, but also asks for more attention. A short page can get to the action sooner, but may leave fit and use questions unresolved. Neither is a universal winner; the appropriate depth depends on the visitor and route into the page.

04 / What the work established

A testable design space.
A more useful conversation.

The campaign set turns broad creative opinions into concrete alternatives: who the page speaks to, what it emphasizes, how much it explains, and where it asks visitors to go next.

The earlier portfolio records six experiments and a best conversion lift of 239%. The available materials do not link that result to these ego/partner screens or establish a winning story. This case study does not attribute the lift to either treatment.

05 / Reflection

A result becomes useful when you understand the conditions that produced it.

The lasting design lesson is to separate a strong creative direction from evidence that it performs. Campaign screens explain what was tested; the analysis needs to explain what the behavior means.

For future iterations, I would carry the treatment, audience, conversion definition, exposure period, and decision into one experiment record so the team could reuse the learning without losing context.
