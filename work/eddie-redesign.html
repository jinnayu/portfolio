Overview

Trust had to happen before conversion could.

Eddie operates in a category where customers arrive with questions they may not be comfortable asking out loud. The redesign needed to create desire without minimizing the sensitivity of the problem — and reduce uncertainty without burying users in information.

The problem

The product was useful. The experience wasn’t explaining why.

The existing site leaned on familiar med-tech conventions: dense content, clinical visual language, and a product story that required too much effort from the customer. Important questions around how the product worked and how it would fit were not being answered clearly enough.

Create desire.
Reduce uncertainty.

That became the principle behind the redesign. Instead of treating the homepage and PDP as separate pages, I thought about them as one confidence-building journey.

01 · Product storytelling

Giving information an order.

I restructured the experience from problem → product → how it works → why it’s different → proof → purchase. The goal was progressive disclosure: give users the information they need at the moment they need it, rather than front-loading every detail.

A signature product moment

One of the visual anchors became a scroll-driven product sequence inspired by premium consumer-tech storytelling. Instead of adding another paragraph explaining the mechanism, the product itself became part of the explanation.

02 · Sizing confidence

A sizing chart wasn’t enough.

Sizing uncertainty showed up as a practical friction point with consequences beyond the page: hesitation, support questions, and returns. I redesigned the sizing experience around guidance rather than another static reference table.

The new experience combined interactive sizing information, visual fit guidance, educational content, a how-to guide, and video instruction — answering common questions before customers needed to contact support.

Impact

The redesign gave us a better experience — and a better baseline to keep improving.

The launch also established a stronger foundation for the work that followed: continuous experimentation, a scalable component system, and a more cohesive journey across site, landing pages, and lifecycle touchpoints.

Reflection

I learned that a redesign shouldn’t be an ending.

The strongest outcome wasn’t a final collection of screens. It was creating a clearer system for learning what customers needed next. That mindset carried directly into the CRO program and design system that followed.
