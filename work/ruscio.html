Overview

A Meet the Team page is really a trust page.

Prospective patients aren’t only looking for names and headshots. They’re trying to understand who they may trust with their health, what each person actually does, and who they’ll interact with if they become a patient.

The information problem

More people shouldn’t mean more scrolling.

Doctors, clinicians, support staff, and other team members all needed representation — but they didn’t need identical information or identical emphasis. A repeating card grid could hold the content, but it wouldn’t necessarily help users understand the organization.

The page needed to explain
roles before resumes.

01 · Information architecture

Organize around how patients understand the team.

I restructured the page around roles and user intent, strengthening the hierarchy between groups and making individual profiles faster to scan. I explored a tabbed-card navigation model that could separate team categories without forcing users through the entire roster.

Designed to grow

The pattern also solved an operational problem: new people could be added within an existing structure rather than forcing another redesign as the organization expanded.

02 · Visual trust

Let the people carry the page.

Because the surrounding site contains dense health information, this experience needed breathing room. Clear grouping, generous spacing, restrained UI, and consistent profile treatment created a warmer rhythm while keeping the content credible and easy to navigate.

Outcome

Clearer today. Easier to maintain tomorrow.

The redesigned experience created a stronger hierarchy for introducing doctors, staff, and patient-support roles while giving the internal team a reusable structure that can scale as the organization changes.

Reflection

Healthcare UX often means organizing uncertainty.

The interface didn’t need to be louder. It needed to make the right information easier to find, reduce ambiguity, and give users a clearer mental model of the people behind their care.
